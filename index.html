Essential Photoshop Tools

Lasso Tool

Intermediate

Freehand selection for custom boundaries. Includes polygonal lasso (straight lines) and magnetic lasso (auto-detects edges — ideal for cutting out objects with precision).

Quick Selection

Automatically detects and selects objects as you paint over them. Photoshop's AI analyzes edges and textures to create accurate selections effortlessly.

Crop Tool

Beginner

Change composition by trimming and straightening. Drag to set your crop area, then use the top toolbar for manual measurements, aspect ratios, or presets.

Patch Tool

Draw a selection and drag to replace it with a nearby area. Retains detail and texture — ideal for portraits. Includes healing brush and spot-healing brush. Resize with [ and ] keys.

Brush Tool

Eraser Tool

Interactive

Tool Explorer

Click any tool to see its detailed description, tips, and pro techniques.

Move Tool

Keyboard Shortcut: V

The Move Tool is your most fundamental tool. With it active, you can reposition your entire photo within its document or move individual layers when working with multiple stacked elements.

It's the starting point for any composition work — arranging elements, aligning layers, and distributing objects across your canvas.

Pro Tip: Hold Shift while moving to constrain movement to horizontal, vertical, or 45-degree diagonal. Hold Alt/Opt and drag to duplicate a layer instantly.

Rectangular Marquee

Keyboard Shortcut: M

Draw rectangular selections over your photo. Hold Shift to constrain to a square. The sub-menu gives you ovals and circles (again with Shift for perfect circles).

Use it to isolate specific areas for adjustments, crop to a selection, or create geometric masks for creative effects.

Pro Tip: Hold Alt/Opt to draw from center. Combine with Shift+Alt/Opt to draw a perfect square from center. Use Select > Transform Selection to resize after drawing.

Lasso Tool

Keyboard Shortcut: L

Unlike the marquee, the lasso gives you completely freehand selection. Draw any shape you need to select a specific area without being limited to geometric shapes.

The polygonal lasso draws straight-line segments (click point to point), while the magnetic lasso automatically follows edges — perfect for cutting out objects with defined outlines.

Pro Tip: While using the regular lasso, hold Alt/Opt temporarily to switch to polygonal mode for straight segments. Release to go back to freehand.

Crop Tool

Keyboard Shortcut: C

Change composition, aspect ratio, and canvas size in one operation. Place your cursor at the top-left, drag to set the crop boundary. The options bar offers presets, manual measurements, and units.

Straighten crooked photos by dragging the straighten slider, or click the Straighten icon and draw a line along what should be horizontal or vertical.

Pro Tip: Enable "Content-Aware" fill in the crop options to intelligently fill empty areas when straightening or extending the canvas.

Patch Tool

Keyboard Shortcut: J

The patch tool removes blemishes and unwanted elements by sampling from another area. Draw a selection around the problem, then drag to a clean area — Photoshop blends the texture seamlessly.

The healing brush (same sub-menu) does this at a brush level, while the spot-healing brush automatically finds the best source. All three retain texture and detail far better than the clone stamp.

Pro Tip: Work on a separate empty layer with "Sample All Layers" enabled. This keeps your corrections non-destructive and easy to undo individually.

Brush Tool

Keyboard Shortcut: B

Your primary painting tool — paints colored strokes and is essential for working with layer masks. Right-click anywhere to adjust size and hardness. The top menu bar offers blend mode control for how each stroke interacts with the layer below.

The sub-menu includes a mixer brush (simulates real paint mixing) and pencil tool (hard-edged strokes). Both are useful for digital illustration and pixel art.

Pro Tip: When painting layer masks, use white to reveal and black to conceal. Adjust brush opacity (number keys 1-0) for gradual masking effects.

Clone Stamp

Keyboard Shortcut: S

Hold Alt/Opt to set a source point (crosshair appears), then paint over the area you want to cover. Unlike the patch tool, the clone stamp copies pixels exactly, which can flatten texture — sometimes desirable for an airbrushed look.

Best used for areas where you need exact pixel-level control over what replaces the original content. Multiple clicks build up the effect.

Pro Tip: Change the clone source frequently to avoid creating visible patterns. Lower the opacity to 30-50% and build up gradually for natural results.

Eraser Tool

Keyboard Shortcut: E

Removes pixels to reveal transparency or the layer below. Powerful when layering two images and revealing features underneath — commonly used for old/new photo juxtapositions.

Right-click to change size, hardness, and even shape — Photoshop lets you use any brush head as an eraser, which opens up creative possibilities.

Pro Tip: In most professional workflows, prefer layer masks over the eraser. Masks are non-destructive — you can always paint back what you've hidden.

Dodge & Burn

Keyboard Shortcut: O

Fine-tune exposure using techniques from the darkroom era. Dodge lightens areas, Burn darkens them. The key settings are Range (which tones to affect) and Exposure (how strong each stroke is).

Dodge on highlights makes white areas pure white. Dodge on midtones brightens skin. Burn on shadows deepens contrast and creates drama. Start at 10-15% exposure and layer strokes.

Pro Tip: Create a 50% gray layer set to Overlay blend mode. Paint with white to dodge and black to burn — fully non-destructive and adjustable with opacity.

Type Tool

Keyboard Shortcut: T

Add text to your design in horizontal or vertical orientation. Draw a text area, type your content, then customize font, weight, size, and color from the options bar. Press the tick icon to confirm.

Text lives on its own layer and remains fully editable. Use the Move tool to reposition, and the Character/Paragraph panels for fine-grained typographic control.

Pro Tip: Convert text to a shape (Type > Convert to Shape) for advanced path editing — but note this makes the text no longer editable as characters.

Shapes

Keyboard Shortcut: U

Draw vector shapes with customizable fill and stroke. Choose from rectangles, ellipses, polygons, lines, and custom shapes. The options bar controls fill color, outline, and thickness independently.

Shapes are vector-based, so they scale without quality loss. Create custom shapes or download community libraries to expand your collection.

Pro Tip: Hold Shift while drawing to constrain proportions. Hold Space while drawing to reposition the shape before releasing. Combine shapes with path operations (union, subtract, intersect).

Advanced

Professional Workflow

Master these advanced techniques to elevate your work from hobbyist to professional.

Non-Destructive Editing

Always use adjustment layers, smart objects, and layer masks. Never edit pixels directly — keep every decision reversible.

Smart Objects

Embed files that scale without quality loss. Apply smart filters that remain fully editable. Link multiple instances that update together.

Blending Modes

Multiply darkens, Screen lightens, Overlay adds contrast. Master these to combine layers with natural-looking interactions instead of simple opacity.

Actions & Automation

Record repetitive tasks as Actions, then batch-process hundreds of files. Create droplets for drag-and-drop automation of entire folders.

Camera RAW

Process RAW files with maximum quality. Adjust white balance, exposure, tone curves, and lens corrections before any pixel-level editing begins.

Color Management

Calibrate your monitor, work in the right color profile (sRGB for web, Adobe RGB for print), and soft-proof before output.

Masking Techniques

Luminosity masks for tonal control, channel-based masks for precision hair selections, and vector masks for hard-edged geometric shapes.

Export & Delivery

Use Export As for web-optimized assets. Save for Web (Legacy) for pixel-perfect control. Generate assets automatically from layer names.

Color Science

Color Spaces & Theory

Choosing the right color model determines whether your work looks correct on screen and in print.

RGB

Red, Green, Blue — additive color model for digital screens. Mixes light to create colors. Always use RGB for web, UI, social media, and any screen-based design.

CMYK

Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key — subtractive model for print. Uses ink to mix colors. Convert to CMYK before sending files to a professional printer. Colors may shift from screen preview.

sRGB vs Adobe RGB

sRGB is the universal web standard — what browsers and most displays show. Adobe RGB captures a wider gamut with more saturated greens and cyans — use it for professional photography and high-end print work.

Typography

Font Pairing
& Selection

The right typeface carries 90% of a design's personality. These three rules guide every font decision.

Limit to Two Fonts

Choose one dominant font for headings and one highly readable font for body text. More than two fonts compete for attention and fracture visual identity.

Create Contrast

Pair opposing styles — a bold geometric sans-serif heading with a classic serif body. Contrast builds hierarchy. Avoid pairing two fonts that are too similar.

Match the Mood

Sharp, geometric fonts signal tech and modernity. Elegant thin serifs convey luxury. Soft, rounded fonts feel friendly and casual. Let the typeface carry the brand's voice.
